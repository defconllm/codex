A chest is not a house, and Arculus is not posted on the house. The house has a door and the door has its own guards, and every morning at the salutatio those guards stand aside and let a queue of clients, creditors and near-strangers into the atrium — which is the room where the chest is. That is not the door failing. That is what the door is for.

arculus circulus quod ubique arceat

A little circle that wards off, on every side. Ubique is the load-bearing word in the gloss. The circle is not drawn between the box and the street, or between the box and the door; it is drawn around the box, closed, so that the direction a hand arrives from stops being a question anyone has to answer. Festus records him in one line — the god who bore the guardianship of caskets — and one line is the correct length. He has one job.

The name needs both of its roots. Arceo is to hold off, keep at a distance, refuse: the plain verb for denying access. Arcus is a bow, an arch, anything bent round until it meets itself. Together they give a boundary with a radius of about one chest — far too small to be a perimeter, which is the whole of the point.

Augustine's joke about the doorway was that one porter is enough for a house, so why three gods. He never got as far as the box, and the box has a different answer: the porter is at the door, and the box is in the room the porter has been letting people into since dawn.

He stops for

  • A hand that came in with the morning queue and knows which box it wants.
  • A key of the right shape on the wrong belt.
  • The whole chest carried off to be opened somewhere quiet.

He does not stop for

  • The doors being shut. He was never posted on the doors.
  • Standing. An invitation to the atrium is an invitation to the atrium.
  • The argument that a lock is already a lock.

Where the box was kept

Not the back room. Not a cellar with one key.

The arca stood in the atrium: ironclad oak, bolted through the floor, holding the deeds, the wills, the contracts and the ready money. Rome kept its most portable wealth in its least private room, in plain view of everyone it did business with.

That reads as carelessness until you price the alternative. A strongbox nobody can reach is a strongbox nobody can use, and the business was done in the atrium precisely because it was done in front of witnesses. The chest is in public because the transaction is public. The contents are not, and nothing about the room was ever asked to make them private.

This page is the same argument, which is why he is on it rather than the door. The file you are reading is built to be pushed to a repository anyone can clone. The repository is the atrium. The word is Arculus.

Before the lid lifts

Never what you want out of it. That is your business. He asks one thing, and the number that matters is not one.

Is that your key?

The doorway asks at the doorway because a threshold is crossed once and then it is behind you. A chest is not crossed. It is opened, and shut, and opened again, all day, by whoever is standing next to it — and standing next to it was granted this morning to everyone who came for the salutatio. So the question is not answered and retired. It is put on every lift of the lid, and the hand that answered it an hour ago answers it again now, on the same terms, with nothing carried over from last time.

The clavis was a good lock: a bronze bit bent square, teeth cut to raise the tumblers clear of the bolt so the bolt could slide back. It is also a shape, and a shape can be copied by anyone with a legitimate reason to stand within arm's length of the thing being copied. Arculus is the part still working after the key is on the wrong belt.

The five bindings

What an arca ferrata actually was, taken apart. Four of them are carpentry.

Sheath
Oak frame under iron plate on every face, so force arrives at metal instead of timber. Metal is slow and loud, and both of those are the point.
Bolt
A rod driven through the base into the stone, or the whole chest sunk until its lid sits flush with the floor. What cannot be carried cannot be opened somewhere quiet.
Tumbler
Lift and slide: the bit rises, the tumblers clear the bolt, the bolt draws back. Access is a mechanism and not a permission.
Sight
Kept in the open, in the busiest room in the house. A box everyone can see is a box everyone can see being opened.
Circle
Arculus. Not on the timber, the iron or the lock — on the contents, and still there when the first four have been picked.

The first four are what you do about a room full of people. The fifth is what you do about the first four. It is also the only one that still means anything once the box has been copied rather than opened, which is the failure the other four were never built for and cannot be made to cover.

Then he goes back to the chest, because the chest is where he lives.
